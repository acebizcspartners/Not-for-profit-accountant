FAQ

What boards ask before they engage us.

When is the AIS actually due?

Six months after the end of your financial year. Most Australian charities run a 30 June year-end, so the AIS is due 31 December. Some charities use a different year-end (e.g. December) and that shifts the AIS deadline accordingly.

We're behind on lodgement. What's the risk?

One late AIS goes on your public ACNC record but isn't fatal. Two consecutive late or missing AIS submissions and the ACNC can revoke your registration — at which point you lose your tax concessions (TCC, ITEC, GST concessions, DGR, FBT cap). We can usually catch a behind charity up inside two weeks.

SPFS vs GPFS — what's the difference?

Special Purpose Financial Statements (SPFS) follow only the accounting standards your charity chooses to apply. General Purpose Financial Statements (GPFS) follow full Australian Accounting Standards. SPFS is simpler and cheaper to prepare, but only available where there are no external users beyond members and the ACNC. We confirm which applies before drafting.

Do you do the audit yourselves?

No — we're tax agents and accountants, not auditors. We prepare the financial statements and working papers, and we liaise with your audit firm. If you don't already have an auditor we can introduce you to firms we work with regularly.

What if our books are in a mess?

Common situation, especially with volunteer treasurers and turnover on the board. We do a clean-up engagement first — bank recs, GST recs, payroll, restricted-fund splits — then build the financials on a tidy trial balance. Quoted up front, fixed fee.

Do you cover NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA?

Yes. Our office is in Norwest NSW but we work cloud-native with charities and incorporated associations in every state. State-specific incorporated association returns (NSW Fair Trading, CAV VIC, OFT QLD, ConsumerProtection WA, CBS SA) are part of our standard scope.
