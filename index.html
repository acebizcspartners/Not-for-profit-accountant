FAQ

Straight answers for boards & treasurers.

Do we really need a specialist NFP accountant?

If you're a registered charity, incorporated association or company limited by guarantee — yes. NFPs have their own reporting framework (ACNC), their own concessions (FBT cap, GST, income tax exemption), their own audit thresholds and their own donor receipting rules. Generalists miss things, which exposes your board personally to compliance risk.

What's the ACNC Annual Information Statement (AIS)?

The AIS is the annual return every ACNC-registered charity must lodge — due six months after your financial year end (so 31 December for a 30 June year-end). It captures your activities, beneficiaries, finances and governance. Miss it twice and the ACNC can revoke your registration, which takes your tax concessions with it.

Do we need an audit?

Depends on size. ACNC classifies charities as Small (under $500k revenue), Medium ($500k–$3M) or Large ($3M+). Large charities need audited financial statements. Medium charities need at least a review engagement. Small charities typically don't need either — though some state regulators or grant funders require it regardless of ACNC size.

What's DGR endorsement and can we get it?

Deductible Gift Recipient (DGR) endorsement lets donors claim tax deductions on gifts to you — a major fundraising advantage. Eligibility depends on your activities matching a DGR category (PBI, HPC, public library, environmental organisation, religious instruction, etc.). We assess eligibility, prepare the application and assemble the evidence pack.

How does FBT work for charities?

Registered Public Benevolent Institutions (PBIs) and Health Promotion Charities (HPCs) can salary-package up to $15,900 in concessional fringe benefits per employee per FBT year without FBT — plus a separate meal/entertainment cap. This is a huge staff retention tool. We set up the packaging, calculate FBT (if any) and lodge the return.

What's the difference between Special Purpose and General Purpose financial statements?

Special Purpose Financial Statements (SPFS) are simpler — fine for smaller charities with no external users beyond members and ACNC. General Purpose Financial Statements (GPFS) follow full Australian Accounting Standards and are required for larger NFPs or where there are external users (donors, funders, public). We work out which tier applies and prepare accordingly.
